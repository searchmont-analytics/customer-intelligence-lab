Recommended research response

Proposed plan · Not an OLG initiative

Do not treat an observed satisfaction gap as a diagnosis. Build an evidence-based decision with the following workstreams.

01 · Diagnose

Validate the performance signal

Audit question consistency, survey coverage, changes in customer mix and uncertainty. Establish a weighted tracking baseline by channel.

02 · Understand

Investigate experience barriers

Use stratified surveys, interviews and task-based research. Triangulate self-reported friction with consented, purpose-limited journey data.

03 · Evaluate

Test improvements safely

Assess product information clarity, accessibility and customer support. Measure comprehension, service success and satisfaction alongside player-health safeguards.

Non-negotiable guardrail: protect player well-being, informed choice and accessibility; do not use vulnerability, loss or intensity to increase gambling. Any intervention must pass privacy, responsible gaming and research ethics review.

Research Methodology Lab

Module 02 · Design evidence that supports decisions

Interactive prototype
Research mandate

Is declining satisfaction a genuine change in experience, or a change in who answers our survey?

Define the target population, validate measures, calibrate the sample and quantify uncertainty before recommending an intervention. All experiments below are fictional, not estimates of OLG customers.

01 · Questionnaire design

Proposed split-ballot experiment: randomly assign respondents to one of two wordings, holding timing and response scale constant.

Leading wording

How satisfied are you with our improved and convenient service?

Presupposes improvement and primes a positive response.

Neutral wording

Thinking about your most recent service interaction, how satisfied or dissatisfied were you?

Very dissatisfied / dissatisfied / neither / satisfied / very satisfied / not applicable.

Proposed approach: cognitive interviews, accessibility review, randomize ballot assignment, and compare response distributions and missingness. Keep historical wording unchanged for the tracking series or run a bridge study. No assumed wording effect is presented as observed.

02 · Sampling and calibration

Controlled synthetic scenario: digital satisfaction 80%, retail 60%. Target population mix is illustrative. Change the composition and completed sample size.

StratumSample shareTarget shareCalibration weightAssumed CSAT

Weight = target share / sample share. Kish effective n = (sum w)² / sum w². This measures unequal-weight dispersion, not every design effect. Coverage gaps, nonresponse within strata and unknown selection probabilities can still bias estimates. Weighting cannot establish representativeness.

03 · Cross-tabulation and independent-wave significance

Uses the same 2,400 fictional records as Module 1. Compare product or service satisfaction across channels within a wave, then compare independent waves 1 and 4.

ChannelnSatisfiedNot satisfiedRate95% Wilson interval
Two-sided pooled two-proportion z-test, independent unweighted observations, alpha 0.05, unadjusted exploratory comparison. Require all expected cells ≥5. Statistical significance is not practical significance or causation; do not use this test for matched panel members or complex survey samples.

04 · Tracking and composition effects

Standardize each fictional wave to the same illustrative 40% digital / 60% retail mix. Compare raw and standardized results to investigate the effect of changing channel composition.

WaveDigital shareDigital CSATRetail CSATRaw CSATStandardized CSAT

Standardization is descriptive, not causal; fixed reference shares are assumptions, not known OLG population totals. Independent waves are repeated cross-sections, not individual longitudinal trajectories.

05 · Research approval and tabulation plan

Design gate

Define eligible adult population, frame, consent, accessible questionnaire, sampling approach, nonresponse follow-up and hypotheses. Include non-digital recruitment to avoid coverage gaps.

Analysis gate

Predefine satisfaction top-two-box denominator, missing/NA treatment, channel and wave banners, sample bases, weighting targets, minimum cells (30), practical change threshold and multiplicity control.

Release gate

Peer review code and tables, reconcile totals, disclose limitations and uncertainty, archive instrument and versioned tabulation plan. Assess player health, privacy and accessibility before action.

Proposed executive decision rule: investigate a validated decline of at least 3 percentage points, corroborated with qualitative evidence; set the threshold with stakeholders before fieldwork. Do not infer causes or approve deployment from this simulation.

Customer Segmentation Explorer

Module 03 · Turn differences in customer needs into testable experience strategies

Interactive prototype
Executive research question

Do customers experience the same service differently because their needs differ?

Build needs-based segments, evaluate their reliability, and translate the differences into research priorities. Segment design serves accessibility, service quality and informed choices — never gambling intensity or promotional targeting.

01 · Design the segmentation instrument

Synthetic data

Proposed question: How important is each of the following when interacting with a gaming-related customer service? Rate on a 1–7 scale (1 = not at all important; 7 = extremely important), with a separate not-applicable option in a real survey.

The existing 2,400 fictional records are extended with six simulated ratings from four overlapping, intentionally planted response patterns plus noise and modest barrier-related shifts. There are no real customer responses. Satisfaction, channel, demographics and actual gambling activity are not clustering inputs; channel and satisfaction are descriptive profiling variables only. Validate questionnaire reliability and coverage before using this approach with real adults.

02 · Fit and validate the clustering model

Standardized six-dimensional ratings · Euclidean K-means++ · five initializations per candidate · k = 2–6.

kWithin-cluster sum of squaresSilhouetteStability (ARI)Smallest shareQuality gate
Silhouette: average cohesion versus separation on a fixed random sample of 320 records (−1 to +1, higher is better). Stability: mean pairwise adjusted Rand index (ARI) across three independently refitted models, each trained on a different 75% subsample of the training pool; evaluated on the same held-out 25% of records (600), with training-specific scaling. ARI near 1 = agreement; near 0 = chance-level agreement. This measures sensitivity to these resamples, not future population stability. The optimal WCSS cannot increase as k increases; finite-start K-means can find local optima. Avoid selecting k from WCSS alone. Low or nearly tied silhouette scores indicate overlapping groups or uncertainty; compare practical interpretability and validate on a new independent sample before operational use. Gates are illustrative, not industry standards.

03 · Visualize the selected solution

Principal component analysis (PCA) reduces six standardized features to two coordinates. Colours identify the calculated cluster memberships.

05 · From research to business decisions

Validate

Test questionnaire design, compare alternative models, repeat on new survey waves, evaluate sampling and weighting, and corroborate with interviews.

Activate responsibly

Use findings to prioritize inclusive service design, information clarity and protective tools. Do not infer risk, diagnose individuals, or promote gambling based on segment membership.

Measure

Define task completion, customer satisfaction, accessibility and player-health measures with stakeholders; evaluate changes with pre-registered research designs.

Segment names are generated from the highest relative feature mean, not from hard-coded personas. This exercise demonstrates the workflow, not verified OLG segments or market shares.
