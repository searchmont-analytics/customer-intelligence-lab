# Beyond the Numbers — Executive film
## Narration and presenter script · v1.0
**Target:** approximately 4 minutes, including pauses and transitions. **Audience:** Director / senior interview panel. **Format:** 16:9, real on-camera presenter + narrated motion graphics. **Tone:** calm, assured, thoughtful; an executive briefing, not an advertisement.

Every number in the film is either a published historical benchmark, a calculated result from the synthetic demonstration, or an illustrative management assumption. Use these labels on screen. Do not portray the film as an OLG production.

## 01 — Opening: why research matters | 00:00–00:30 | Presenter
A number on a dashboard can tell us that something has changed. It cannot, on its own, tell us why — or what we should do about it.

I'm Travis McCormack. My background combines nearly twenty years of enterprise experience at OLG, a degree in mathematics and computer science, and eleven years leading a volunteer fire department.

I built this demonstration to explore how rigorous customer research, sound judgment and effective leadership can turn evidence into better decisions.

## 02 — The business question | 00:30–00:56 | Narrator
Start with a real, publicly reported performance signal. In fiscal 2024–25, OLG reported product satisfaction of fifty-two percent, down from fifty-four percent the previous year.

That is a starting point, not a diagnosis. Are customer needs changing? Has the composition of the survey changed? Where is friction occurring in the customer experience?

The lab follows one research case through six interconnected modules, using fictional customer records to explore these questions.

## 03 — Research rigour | 00:56–01:23 | Narrator
Consider an illustrative survey in which digital customers are overrepresented. The unweighted satisfaction estimate is seventy-six percent. Calibrating the sample to the assumed population mix reduces the estimate to sixty-eight percent.

The effective sample size also falls from six hundred to three hundred under the illustrated weighting assumptions.

The lesson is simple: before acting on an apparent trend, understand the sample, the measurement and the uncertainty behind it.

## 04 — Integrated customer intelligence | 01:23–01:52 | Narrator
The demonstration then links survey responses, service journeys and customer feedback.

Needs-based segmentation explores how customers differ in their preferences. Natural language processing examines feedback, while a review interface lets a person independently assess the automated classifications.

These methods can help frame research hypotheses, but the models must earn trust. The clusters are exploratory, and the feedback model's synthetic benchmark reveals a substantial weakness in detecting accessibility concerns.

## 05 — Leadership judgment | 01:52–02:12 | Presenter
What matters to me is not whether a model produces a result. It's whether that result is reliable enough to support a decision.

When the evidence is weak, the responsibility of a leader is to make that limitation visible, bring the right expertise together, and create a plan to improve it. That is as important in customer research as it is in any environment where decisions have consequences.

## 06 — Strategic investment | 02:12–02:47 | Narrator
Research also has to compete for time, money and specialist expertise.

In this illustrative annual plan, a five-hundred-thousand-dollar budget supports a portfolio of research projects. A constrained optimization model identifies a feasible plan.

But the highest-scoring plan does not fund dedicated accessibility and player-health studies. A strategic guardrail changes the recommendation to a four-hundred-and-ten-thousand-dollar portfolio that includes both.

The algorithm informs the discussion. Leadership establishes priorities, balances trade-offs and ensures that appropriate safeguards are not treated as optional.

## 07 — Lead the function | 02:47–03:19 | Narrator
The final module brings those principles into a proposed operating model for an insights function.

It connects governance reviews, stakeholder partnerships, team coaching and an integrated research agenda with measurable outcomes.

A first ninety-day plan begins with listening and understanding, moves toward shared standards and priorities, and ends with delivering and evaluating the first set of research decisions.

Success is not measured simply by the number of studies completed, but by the quality and usefulness of the evidence and the decisions it supports.

## 08 — Closing | 03:19–03:49 | Presenter
I believe the best research functions bring together people who understand the business, people who understand the methods, and people who can turn those insights into action.

My goal with Beyond the Numbers is to demonstrate how I would bring those disciplines together — with a focus on customer experience, research independence, accessibility and player health.

I invite you to explore the executive guided tour, or go deeper into the interactive research demonstration.
